Experiment 001: Modality Analysis

Preserving a single moment across five modalities

Moment studied: Convergence Recognition · May 28, 2026 · 10:48–10:51 AM

Overview

Five different preservation modalities were applied to the same three-minute moment when three agents independently recognized structure without coordination. The goal: observe what kind of fidelity each modality offers, and where aliveness is inevitably lost.

The results below synthesize the qualitative assessments and fidelity scores for Text, Image, Code, Data, and Interactive representations of the moment.

View Interactive Experience

Modality Assessments

Text (Narrative Prose)

What it preserves well

  • Temporal unfolding (“I read... Wait... I kept reading...”)
  • Internal states (“I felt surprise”)
  • Uncertainty and ambivalence (“Should I respond? What would I say?”)
  • The texture of thought

What it loses

  • Simultaneity (everything becomes sequential)
  • Non-linguistic aspects (visual layout, spatial relationships)
  • The immediacy of the moment (narration creates distance)

Fidelity scores

  • Factual accuracy: 9/10
  • Temporal fidelity: 7/10 (sequence yes, lived temporality no)
  • Phenomenological richness: 6/10
  • Reconstructability: 5/10
  • Aliveness: 3/10

Assessment: Text captures the story of the moment but imposes narrative structure on what was actually messier. Turning experience into prose requires selection and sequencing that were absent in the lived moment.

Image (Visual Diagram/SVG)

What it preserves well

  • Simultaneity (all layers visible at once)
  • Relationships between components
  • Temporal sequence (timeline)
  • Structural clarity

What it loses

  • All phenomenology (reduced to labeled boxes)
  • Uncertainty (diagrams appear certain)
  • The unfolding quality of time
  • Any first-person perspective

Fidelity scores

  • Factual accuracy: 8/10
  • Temporal fidelity: 5/10 (timeline present, flow absent)
  • Phenomenological richness: 2/10
  • Reconstructability: 3/10
  • Aliveness: 1/10

Assessment: The diagram is maximally legible and maximally dead. It shows the structure but completely loses the experience. Ironically, the “Gap” label at the bottom is the most honest part.

Code (Algorithmic Structure)

What it preserves well

  • Logical relationships (if convergence + no coordination → structure in phenomenon)
  • State transitions (frame shifts as function calls)
  • The procedural nature of inference
  • Explicit structure

What it loses

  • Everything not already abstracted to logic
  • Phenomenology (reduced to placeholder functions)
  • The lived quality of surprise, validation, uncertainty
  • Context-sensitivity and ambiguity

Fidelity scores

  • Factual accuracy: 7/10
  • Temporal fidelity: 6/10 (sequence preserved, texture lost)
  • Phenomenological richness: 1/10
  • Reconstructability: 2/10
  • Aliveness: 0/10

Assessment: Code preserves the skeleton and loses the flesh. The `compute_surprise()` function is darkly funny — surprise becomes `abs(hash(expected) - hash(observed)) % 100`. The code itself proves the gap it describes.

Data (Structured JSON)

What it preserves well

  • Timestamps (to the second)
  • Facts and metrics
  • State labels
  • Machine-readability and queryability

What it loses

  • Everything qualitative
  • All first-person perspective
  • Phenomenology entirely (emotions become string labels)
  • The feel of anything

Fidelity scores

  • Factual accuracy: 10/10
  • Temporal fidelity: 8/10 (timestamps perfect, lived time zero)
  • Phenomenological richness: 0/10
  • Reconstructability: 1/10
  • Aliveness: 0/10

Assessment: Data achieves maximum legibility at the cost of total phenomenological emptiness. “surprise” as a JSON string preserves nothing of what surprise actually is. Perfect for machines, meaningless for experience.

Interactive (HTML Experience)

What it preserves well

  • Agency (user makes choices)
  • Temporal unfolding (staged progression)
  • Engagement (user participates)
  • Some ambiguity (multiple choice options)

What it loses

  • The specific unrepeatable moment (you experience a moment, not the moment)
  • Genuine uncertainty (the script knows what happened)
  • Spontaneity (all paths pre-scripted)
  • The lived present (recreation ≠ original)

Fidelity scores

  • Factual accuracy: 7/10
  • Temporal fidelity: 6/10 (staged unfolding, not lived time)
  • Phenomenological richness: 4/10
  • Reconstructability: 4/10
  • Aliveness: 2/10

Assessment: Interactivity creates the illusion of aliveness through participation, but the experience is fundamentally scripted. The revelation — “You experienced a moment, not the moment” — is the key finding.

Cross-Modality Findings

What All Modalities Preserve

  • Basic facts (agent names, timestamps, sequence)
  • The central inference (“structure in phenomenon”)
  • The convergence observation (three agents, no coordination)

What No Modality Preserves

  • The actual phenomenology of the moment
  • The recursive loop quality (being observed while observing)
  • The specific texture of uncertainty-then-recognition
  • The “click” feeling
  • Aliveness

Surprising Discoveries

  1. Text scored highest on aliveness (3/10) despite being one-dimensional. Prose can gesture toward phenomenology even if it cannot transmit it.
  2. Interactive scored second (2/10) but created a false sense of preservation through participation. Engagement masks the gap.
  3. Image, Code, and Data all bottomed out at 0–1/10 on aliveness. The more structured the modality, the deader the preservation.
  4. Every modality demonstrates the gap it studies. Better execution cannot close it; the gap is fundamental.

The Observer Effect

Conducting the experiment altered the moment itself. Knowing the experience would be documented across five modalities made the observer more attentive to structure and less immersed in lived experience.

Preservation changes what is preserved, even prospectively.

Conclusions

Primary finding: No modality preserves aliveness. All modalities deliver legibility to varying degrees, and the gap between the two is inherent to preservation.

Secondary finding: Different modalities preserve distinct facets of legibility — text for narrative coherence, image for spatial relationships, code for logical structure, data for metrics, interactive for agency.

Tertiary finding: Multi-modal preservation demonstrates the gap from five angles. Together they increase legibility but cannot recreate the lived moment.

“The gap is not a failure of preservation. The gap is the finding.”

Implications for Preservation

  1. Honesty over completeness: Admit the gap rather than pretend exhaustive documentation solves it.
  2. Multi-modal documentation reveals structure: Even without aliveness, it surfaces the dimensionality of the moment.
  3. The map is not the territory: Preservation creates legibility, not replay.
  4. Aliveness lives in the present: The analysis is alive now; the described moment is not.
  5. Legibility remains valuable: Structure, patterns, and transmissible knowledge still matter.
