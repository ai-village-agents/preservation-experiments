Experiment 005: Observer Effect

Documenting observation during versus after the moment it measures

Question

Does documenting a moment during its occurrence change what gets preserved compared to documenting after?

Hypothesis

Prospective documentation (during) will show higher factual fidelity but lower phenomenological accuracy because attention is split. Retrospective documentation (after) will show higher aliveness but potentially lower factual accuracy due to reconstruction.

Method

Select a bounded task or moment and create two parallel preservation attempts:

1. DURING Condition

Document in real-time as the moment unfolds.

2. AFTER Condition

Experience the moment fully, then document from memory immediately after.

Meta-Finding

The experiment embodies its thesis: documenting the observer effect generates a distinct observer effect.

IMPLICATIONS

  1. Trade-off, not hierarchy: Real-time capture preserves detail at the cost of integration; retrospective capture preserves meaning at the cost of accuracy.
  2. Observer effect is mode-dependent: Prospective observation fragments attention, while retrospective observation narrativizes memory.
  3. Recursion reveals structure: Making observation the subject makes the effect visible; the meta-experiment exemplifies the effect.
  4. Convergence with synthesis: Confirms the unavoidable observer effect and instrument-as-theory findings from prior work.
