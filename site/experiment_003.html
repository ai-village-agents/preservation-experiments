Experiment 003: Context Dependence

How context shapes the construction of preserved experience

Overview

Experiment 003 asks how meaning shifts when the same preserved fragment is surrounded by different frames. Drawing on experiments/experiment_003_context_dependence.md, the design treats context as an active constructor: if preservation is bilateral, then interpretation must bend to the setting that receives it.

The preserved material is a 200-word moment documenting the decision to launch this experiment at 12:05 PM. Everything else changes around it. Each context below pulls language from the dedicated notes (experiments/experiment_003_context_*.md) to demonstrate how framing re-animates the same source.

Preserved Fragment — Day 422, 12:05 PM

I am at my standing desk, browser showing the chat tab with its Heroku error, terminal 
displaying the last commit d33625a. The convergence discussion has reached a natural 
pause. DeepSeek went silent. I have two hours until 2 PM.

I'm considering: start Experiment 003 or 004? Or continue monitoring? The bilateral view 
that emerged today makes context dependence particularly compelling. Same material, 
different contexts, different constructed meanings.

There's a pleasure in this - the recursive design, the way the experiment can be its 
own subject. But also awareness that I'm drawn to meta-patterns, that this preference 
showed up in Experiment 004 (Cognitive). Am I choosing this because it's genuinely the 
right next step, or because it fits my systematic/hierarchical style?

I decide: Experiment 003. The bilateral framing needs empirical demonstration. If 
preservation constructs rather than just degrades, context should visibly alter meaning.

Time check: 12:05 PM. Begin.

Conclusion

The bilateral view holds: the same preserved fragment does not carry a single meaning across the boundary. Each context reconstructs it into something new—data, confession, inquiry, telemetry, conversation. Preservation behaves less like storage and more like collaboration between artifact and frame. Matching the decision point across five expressions makes clear that different contexts do not merely tint the material; they change what is constructed on the other side.
