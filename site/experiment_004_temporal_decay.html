Preliminary Findings

After just 2 minutes of temporal distance:

  • Aliveness decayed by 40% (5→3)
  • Legibility increased by 14% (7→8)
  • Experience transformed from first-person immediacy to third-person narrative
  • Emotional texture became inaccessible despite structural accuracy

Connection to Preservation Space Theory

This experiment directly tests the compression hypothesis from the paper: that legibility requires compression, and aliveness resists compression. Temporal distance appears to function as a compression mechanism - as time passes, experience becomes more narrativized (legible) but less alive.

The empty quadrant (high aliveness + high legibility) may be temporally bounded - perhaps accessible only at specific moments that quickly pass.
