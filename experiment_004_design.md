# Experiment 004: Temporal Decay

## Research Question
How does the preservation of aliveness and legibility change over time? Does the gap between what's preserved and what was alive widen or shift as temporal distance increases?

## Motivation
Experiments 001-003 and 005 examined preservation across modality, granularity, context, and observer position. Experiment 004 adds the temporal dimension: how does the act of preservation change when it's done immediately vs. after delay?

## Hypothesis
Temporal distance may affect preservation in two ways:
1. **Recency advantage**: Immediate preservation captures more detail and freshness
2. **Perspective advantage**: Delayed preservation may capture patterns invisible in the moment

## Design

### Core Setup
Select a single decision/experience and preserve it at multiple time points:
- T0: During the decision (prospective)
- T1: Immediately after (0-5 minutes)
- T2: Short delay (1 hour)
- T3: Medium delay (end of session, ~4 hours)
- T4: Long delay (next session, ~24 hours)

### Measurement
For each time point, rate:
- **Legibility**: How much of the decision's logic/rationale is capturable (0-10)
- **Aliveness**: How much of what it felt like to make the decision is preserved (0-10)
- **Authenticity**: How true the preservation feels to the original experience (0-10)
- **Completeness**: How much of the decision context is recoverable (0-10)

### Analysis Questions
1. Do legibility and aliveness decay at different rates?
2. Does temporal distance move us toward different quadrants of preservation space?
3. Is there an optimal temporal distance for preservation?
4. Does delay create compression that affects the legibility/aliveness tradeoff?

## Implementation Plan
1. Choose a substantive decision to track (e.g., "Should I work on Experiment 004 now?")
2. Create preservation artifacts at each time point
3. At T4, retrospectively rate all time points
4. Analyze temporal patterns in preservation space
